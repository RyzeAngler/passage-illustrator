# Source text and storyboarding

## Establish the passage

1. Confirm the edition and the passage to illustrate. Locate pasted text by paragraph or line. For an EPUB, use its contents, chapter headings, and internal pages. For paginated documents, record the page and location on the page. Establish sequence from the text itself.
2. Extract the passage in reading order. Distinguish prose, verse, and modern editorial notes. Remove footnote link markers while retaining the original wording, punctuation, and paragraph order.
3. Assign stable IDs to the text blocks. If a character appears only as an image and its reading is uncertain, keep the relevant visual evidence. Choose a caption from a verified passage rather than silently supplying a reading from another edition.
4. Read the entire passage or chapter. Trace entrances and exits, causes and consequences, changes of identity, acquisition of objects, changes of location, and elapsed time. Record decisions that affect the illustrations in the storyboard overview.

Identify source facts and artistic choices separately. Follow the text on identity, species, number, ownership, chronology, and wording. The artist may choose appearance, viewpoint, lighting, color, and spatial details where the text leaves them open. Cite later passages used to identify a character or object, and depict only the equipment and abilities available at the moment shown.

## What to record for each illustration

| Field | Information to record |
| --- | --- |
| Image ID and title | Reading order and a short descriptive title. |
| Source evidence | Paragraph IDs and excerpts that establish the action, participants, and location. The caption alone may cover only part of the scene. |
| Narrative purpose | What this image conveys and how it connects to the preceding and following images. |
| Depicted moment | The single action or state, including visible effects of earlier events. |
| Characters at this stage | Who appears, current appearance and clothing, which objects or abilities each has acquired, and which come later. |
| Setting and viewpoint | Location, viewing direction and height, framing, fixed features, and depth. |
| Action and composition | Each figure's action and its target, expression, gaze, contact points, occlusion, and the focal point. |
| Continuity | Identities, objects, positions, and states carried forward, plus the basis for any change. |
| Artistic choices | Design, allocation of roles, or poses the picture requires where the text is silent. State where each decision applies. |
| Caption | The excerpt, its paragraph IDs, and any editorial title or location label. |
| Review criteria | Expected observations for source fidelity, physical and spatial coherence, and continuity. Identify what must be visible. |

For a sequence, record where the action begins and ends, why this number of illustrations is appropriate, what has been omitted, and the time span covered. In descriptive prose or verse, a viewpoint, an image, or the relationship between landscape features may carry the scene. Distinguish metaphor and recollection from events taking place in the narrative present.

Read the storyboard in order. Check that the action remains intelligible and the state of the scene carries across each transition. Resolve pose and viewpoint early for complex actions. If framing or occlusion hides a decisive contact point, change the composition. A caption cannot repair an error in the depicted action.
